# Visual checklist: from cue to evidence

Use the most useful rows for the piece; this is not a list of effects every video ought to contain.

| What catches your eye | How to check it | What you can say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A nearly white frame between two images | Step backward and forward. Does the earlier composition return, or does the bright frame bridge two different shots? | Describe a flash, a cut, or both at their respective times. A scene-score peak alone does not decide. |
| A caption changes size, or part of it lands outside the frame | Follow the same letter over consecutive frames. Compare its motion with the background. | Describe the direction, scale change, and the word emphasized. A single cropped letter could simply be the layout. |
| A corner badge stays sharp as the image beneath changes | Compare its edges, position, and visibility across the boundary. Check whether the badge itself changes at a section break. | Identify the graphic as a continuity cue. Do not claim knowledge of the underlying project layers. |
| A foreground cutout and a blurred background appear to move separately | Track two landmarks in each plane over time; look for relative displacement and occlusion. | Report relative motion if observed. A single image with shallow focus does not establish parallax. |
| The image edges repeat or reflect | Compare a border feature to its adjacent interior copy while stepping through the motion. | Report mirrored edge fill if the symmetry holds. The artifact does not identify an application or prove footage origin. |
| The palette switches while the layout stays the same | Compare stable reference areas across the switch; separate a lighting or shot change from an isolated color treatment. | Describe the before/after look and whether the change also marks an editorial boundary. |
| Blurred text, glowing contours, or foreground particles cross other elements | Check direction of blur, halo extent, or which object obscures which. Compare with a held frame. | Describe what is visible and how it directs attention, without guessing a plugin. |
| Cutaways appear to accelerate | Find the first incoming frame of each confirmed cut in two comparable windows. Account separately for holds crossing a window edge. | Compare the verified spacings. To call them beat-synced, check multiple cuts against audible accents and report any misses. |

For pacing, distinguish **shot changes** from **activity inside a shot**. Rapid captions over a locked framing can feel fast without a single cut. Conversely, a fixed overlay can make a sequence of short shots feel continuous.
